InspireMD · Nasdaq: NSPR · Miami, Florida

A carotid stent
with a mesh sleeve

InspireMD describes itself as “a medical device company specializing in the development and commercialization of products for the treatment of carotid artery disease and other vascular conditions.” Its two commercial products, CGuard EPS and CGuard Prime, each combine a self-expanding nitinol stent with a MicroNet mesh sleeve. 10-K, Item 1

The scaffold

A self-expanding nitinol stent

A stent is “an expandable scaffold-like metallic device placed in an artery to widen the lumen and restore blood flow.” This one expands without balloon dilation pressure or an inflation balloon. 10-K, Item 1

The sleeve

MicroNet, a woven polymer mesh

Woven from a single strand of 23 µm polyethylene terephthalate, with pores of 150 to 180 microns, applied to the stent “to provide additional protection” from plaque prolapse and embolization after deployment. 10-K, Item 1

The product

One stent design, three products at different stages

The company says its products treat carotid artery disease, in which plaque builds up in the artery wall and narrows the artery. Plaque debris breaking off and travelling to the brain is, in the 10-K’s words, one of the “significant causes of stroke.” 10-K, Item 1

Sold outside the U.S.

CGuard EPS

The CGuard Carotid Embolic Prevention System joins a self-expanding nitinol stent and a MicroNet mesh sleeve in a single device. It is marketed in over 30 countries outside the United States through a network of distributors.

The mesh, drawn to the stated size

What the 10-K says about the pore

  • The company’s claim. MicroNet “is designed to reduce plaque prolapse and embolic events.” 10-K, Item 1
  • The company’s comparison. It says CGuard “has demonstrated the lowest 30-day and one-year composite adverse event rates compared to historical carotid stent data.” That is a comparison with historical data, not a head-to-head trial. 10-K, Item 1
  • The company’s view of a new use. In acute stroke with tandem lesions, it says the “flexible, low-metal-burden design and MicroNet mesh” may offer advantages where traditional embolic protection cannot be used. That use is in an early feasibility study. 10-Q, Item 2

Market and evidence

The market estimate, and the trials behind the products

The market numbers are the company’s estimates and rest on a 2021 third-party report plus internal estimates. This page adds none of its own.

$1.3B Addressable market estimate

Assuming full penetration of the intervention caseload. The same sources give $9.3 billion for everyone diagnosed with high-grade carotid artery stenosis. The 10-K cites a Health Research International report dated Sep 13, 2021 and internal estimates. 10-K, Item 1

394,000 Carotid interventions in 2022

Of about 3 million people aged 50 to 89 estimated to have been diagnosed with high-grade carotid artery disease, per the same report as cited in the 10-K. 10-K, Item 1

~155,000 Annual U.S. carotid procedures

The figure the 10-K uses for the U.S. opportunity behind its plan to expand its direct salesforce. 10-K, Item 1

Medicare coverage. In October 2023 the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services issued its final national coverage determination, expanding coverage of carotid artery stenting and TCAR to asymptomatic and standard-risk patients. 10-K, Item 1
CREST-2, not a company study. The NIH-sponsored trial across 155 centers reported in November 2025 that stenting plus medical therapy had a significantly lower stroke risk than intensive medical management alone in patients with severe asymptomatic stenosis; in a separate arm, endarterectomy did not show a significant benefit. 10-K, Item 1

Disclosures

What the filings say about risk

Eight matters a reader should have in front of them. Each is in the company’s own filings and carries its source. Figures for the six months ended Jun 30, 2026 and balances at Jun 30, 2026 are unaudited.

01 · Going concern: the company’s conclusion

“we believe we do not have sufficient resources to fund operations for at least the next 12 months. Therefore, there is substantial doubt about our ability to continue as a going concern.”

That is from the Liquidity discussion in the 10-Q for the quarter ended Jun 30, 2026. The same filing reports an accumulated deficit of $330.3M, a net loss of $28.0M and net cash used in operating activities of $23.2M for the six months, against cash and marketable securities of $30.4M. The stated plans are “continued commercialization of our products and raising capital through sale of additional equity securities, debt or capital inflows from strategic partnerships and exercise of warrants.” The company adds that “there are no assurances” it will obtain the financing it needs, and that if it does not it “may need to reduce activities, curtail or cease operations.” The 10-K for 2025, filed Mar 18, 2026, reached the same conclusion, and the report of its auditor, Kesselman & Kesselman, on the 2025 financial statements includes an explanatory paragraph on the company’s ability to continue as a going concern. 10-Q, Liquidity 10-K, Item 1A

02

A U.S. recall of the CGuard Prime delivery system

On May 1, 2026 the company announced a voluntary U.S. recall of the CGuard Prime 135 cm delivery system after a controlled launch showed that its technical success in carotid artery stenting “has not met performance expectations.” The recall covers the delivery system, not the stent implant. The company’s assessment “did not identify new safety concerns for patients who had previously received a CGuard implant,” and by Jun 30, 2026 substantially all affected products had been returned and credits issued. The same release said the company was pausing commercialization of the CGuard Prime delivery system and withdrawing its full-year 2026 revenue guidance. 8-K, May 1, 2026 10-Q, Note 1

In the quarter ended Jun 30, 2026 the recall reduced revenue by $0.73M, U.S. revenue was −$0.35M, and about $0.61M of inventory was impaired; the gross margin was negative 43.7%. The 10-Q says the company cannot assure that the FDA will be satisfied with its corrective actions, that until the issues are resolved to the FDA’s satisfaction it “may be unable to resume commercialization of CGuard Prime in the United States,” and that such requirements could “delay or prevent FDA acceptance or approval” of the proposed PMA supplement, which it expects to submit in the fourth quarter of 2026. 10-Q, Item 2

03

A Nasdaq minimum bid price notice

On Jul 17, 2026 Nasdaq notified the company that it no longer meets the $1.00 minimum bid price in Listing Rule 5550(a)(2), based on the 30 consecutive business days from Jun 3 to Jul 16, 2026. The notice has no immediate effect on trading under the symbol NSPR. The company has 180 calendar days, until Jan 13, 2027, and must close at or above $1.00 for at least ten consecutive business days to regain compliance. The company says it will monitor the bid price and evaluate options to regain compliance, including, if necessary, effecting a reverse stock split. It may be eligible for a second 180-day period if it meets the continued-listing requirement for the market value of publicly held shares and all other initial-listing standards except the bid price, and gives Nasdaq written notice of its intention to cure. 8-K, Jul 23, 2026

The last sale price the 10-Q reports is $0.8401 on Aug 13, 2026. The 10-K lists failure to keep the Nasdaq minimum listing requirements, and the delisting that would follow, among its risks. 10-Q, Liquidity 10-K, Item 1A

04

Dilution: sales of stock and warrants

At-the-market sales. On Apr 3, 2026 the company replaced its 2024 agreement with Piper Sandler with one with BTIG for up to $75 million of common stock at a commission of up to 3.0%. The 10-Q says no shares had been sold under it. 8-K, Apr 3, 2026 10-Q, Liquidity

Warrants. The May 2023 Series J and Series K warrants have an exercise price of $1.3827. By the 10-Q date $33.8M of the $71.4M of gross proceeds possible from all four series had been received, and the company believes holders are unlikely to exercise while the share price is below $1.3827. The Series J warrants run to the earlier of May 15, 2028 and 20 trading days after the company announces FDA approval of SwitchGuard and CGuard Prime 80 cm; the Series K warrants are scheduled to expire Oct 28, 2026. 10-Q, Liquidity

Amendments on Sep 21, 2026. The amendments were made with certain holders of Series J warrants for up to 9,543,952 shares and Series K warrants for up to 9,543,947 shares. For those holders, who include directors, the exercise price became $0.7674 on 50% of their Series J shares and all of their Series K shares, with an end date of the earlier of May 15, 2028 and 20 trading days after the company announces FDA approval of CGuard Prime 80 cm. The company’s headline gave potential gross proceeds of up to $11 million. 8-K, Sep 21, 2026 press release, investor site

Share counts and their bases. 46,921,061 common shares at Jun 30, 2026 and 46,489,118 at Aug 14, 2026, both excluding pre-funded warrants. Authorized common shares are 250,000,000. At Jun 30, 2026 there were also 43,092,107 pre-funded warrants and 25,828,164 Series J and Series K warrants outstanding, and the 46,921,061 shares include 6,734,737 unvested restricted shares. The company closed a private placement of about $40.1M gross in August 2025 and one of $42.2M gross in May 2023. 10-Q, balance sheet 10-Q, cover 10-Q, notes 10-Q, Liquidity

05

Approvals the plan depends on

CGuard EPS has CE Mark approval; its PMA was submitted to the FDA in Q1 2026 and is not approved. CGuard Prime is FDA approved, but the company says it may be unable to resume U.S. commercialization until the recall issues are resolved. The 80 cm version for TCAR and SwitchGuard NPS are in pivotal trials and are not approved. 10-Q, Item 2 The company’s Jun 11, 2026 release, in its note on the 80 cm device, says it submitted a PMA supplement to the FDA that is currently under review; the release does not say which approval the supplement seeks. 8-K, Jun 11, 2026

Among the risks the 10-K lists are that the results of the company’s trials “may be insufficient to obtain regulatory approval,” that the trials needed for a premarket approval “will be lengthy and expensive,” and that its products may be subject to recalls. It also lists that it will need to raise additional capital, and that such capital raising may dilute stockholders. 10-K, Item 1A

06

One factory, in Israel

The company makes CGuard EPS and CGuard Prime at one leased facility of 1,830 square meters in Tel Aviv, Israel, with capacity of about 5,500 products a quarter on a single shift; the lease runs through Dec 31, 2028. The 10-K says that if the facility, its raw materials or its shipping were disrupted it “would have no other means of manufacturing and distributing” the two products until it restored capacity or developed alternatives. 10-K, Item 1A 10-K, Item 2

The 10-K also lists conditions in Israel and its neighbors as a risk, and says a substantial part of revenue is expected in euros and U.S. dollars while a significant part of expenses is in new Israeli shekels. 10-K, Item 1A

07

Changes in leadership and on the board

On Feb 27, 2026 the company gave notice of dismissal to its Chief Operating Officer. On Jul 23, 2026 it told its Chief Commercial Officer that the position had been eliminated, effective Jul 31. On May 20, 2026 the Chair of the Board gave notice of a decision not to seek reelection and to retire at the 2027 annual meeting. Directors resigned effective Dec 31, 2025 and Jul 1, 2026; neither resignation was due to a disagreement with the company. The 10-Q also refers to severance for the former chief financial officer’s retirement, recognized in the quarter ended Jun 30, 2025. After Jun 30, 2026 the company began a workforce reduction expected to cut the number of positions by almost 20%, with annualized operating expense savings of about $9.0 million and aggregate restructuring charges of about $0.9 million to $1.2 million, mostly severance. 8-K, Mar 4, 2026 8-K, Jul 29, 2026 8-K, May 22, 2026 8-K, Dec 19, 2025 8-K, Jul 2, 2026 10-Q, MD&A
